# InfoMapper – Decisions

Condensed record of the decisions taken while designing the prototype (UX, `D-xx`) and the application
(architecture, `AD-xx`). Status as of 2 October 2026. The working documents with full discussion live in Claude;
**this file is the copy the code follows**. When a decision changes, update this file in the same pull request.

Behaviour reference: `docs/prototype/infomapper-prototype-with-sources.html`. Data reference: `docs/data-model-v2.md`.

---

## UX decisions (D-xx)

### Canvas and mappings
- **D-01** The core of the canvas is mapping a source column to an attribute; layers (mappings, relationships) are toggled on one canvas.
- **D-02** A mapping is a model object, independent of any canvas. Removing a card does not remove its mappings.
- **D-03** The canvas is unbounded; the minimap is called “Overview”. The Overview's miniature also draws the visible notes in their paper colour, under the cards.
- **D-36** Attribute order belongs to the model (same on every canvas). Ctrl/Alt + ↑/↓ moves an attribute, Shift to top/bottom; drag in the entity panel. Source columns keep their physical order.
- **D-37** Card width (200–600 px) belongs to the canvas. Long names are truncated in the middle, keeping the part after the last underscore (`customer_addr…_line2`).
- **D-46** New entities: Entity tool (E) and toolbox on the canvas; “+” per concept in the left panel; “New concept”. The concept comes from the concept frame clicked into, else the last used. Duplicate names warn, do not block.
- **D-48** Dragging a column onto an attribute without mappings creates a direct mapping. If the attribute already has a mapping, a choice appears: “Separate mapping (alternative source)” or “Add to mapping …”, pre-selected by a hint (same table: add; other system: separate). Enter confirms, Esc cancels.
- **D-49** A combined mapping: input lines meet in an ƒ node next to the attribute, one line continues; its status colours all its lines. The mapping panel has an Inputs section (order, add, remove); more than one input requires a rule, into which columns are inserted by click. Actions “Split into separate mappings” and “Merge mappings”. The attribute panel shows one row per mapping.
- **D-50** A combined mapping may use columns from different source systems; in Data Vault 2.0 mode the hints warn that it mixes systems (suggest a business rule in a later layer).
- **D-51** Changing an approved mapping's inputs, kind or rule sends it back to review and clears the approval; approval belongs to a version of the mapping. Changing only the note keeps the status. An import sends an approved mapping back to review, with a stored reason, when an input gets a type conflict it did not have, an input with the Business key classification (code BK, D-57) changes type, or a renamed input is named in its rule (the rule is rewritten). The reason is cleared by any change of the mapping's status or content. With four-eyes on, the person who ran the import counts as the mapping's last editor. Moving a mapping's input to another column after its column left the source, and deleting a column that is one input of a combined mapping, send an approved mapping back to review with the reason ("moved from {column} after it left the source", "{column} was deleted from the model"). A change to resolve about a mapping is settled by setting the mapping's status in its panel. (Extended in slices 7a, 7b and 7c.)
- **D-52** A row's right-click menu shows only that row's actions; card actions are only in the card header's menu, so deleting an attribute can't be mistaken for deleting its entity.
- **D-47** Deleting an entity from the model first shows its impact (attributes, mappings incl. approved ones, relationships, canvases, projects, requirements). A concept with entities is deleted only after its entities are moved to another concept; no cascade. Concept frames become free frames.

### Canvases, frames, selection
- **D-04** Frames belong to a canvas, not the model. One model, many canvases.
- **D-05** Dragging an entity into another concept’s frame asks explicitly; membership by drop position; one level of frames.
- **D-06** Drawing or resizing a frame does not take cards from other frames.
- **D-07** A collapsed frame becomes a block; its lines merge into one line per target with a count; layout is unchanged.
- **D-12** Canvas look (background, grid) is per canvas and outside undo.
- **D-14** A frame moves by dragging its name or any empty spot inside it.
- **D-15** Dragging empty canvas draws a lasso. Pan with scroll, Space, middle button; Shift+drag inside a frame draws a lasso.
- **D-16** The lasso selects only elements fully inside it.
- **D-17** A lassoed frame stands for its cards in the selection.
- **D-18** Right-drag pans; the Hand tool (H) pans with the left button.
- **D-19** Right-click without moving opens the toolbox for what is under the pointer (Ctrl+click on Mac, menu key). Right-clicking an unselected item selects it first.
- **D-22** Layer mode is per canvas; notation (crow’s foot / UML) is shared; Focus is momentary and not saved.
- **D-23** No nested frames for now.

### Working layer (labels, notes, context)
- **D-08** Working labels (e.g. `CR-23`) are stored apart from model tags (BK, PII).
- **D-09** Labels are assigned by hand first; automatic labeling is an option.
- **D-10** A live label canvas shows only labeled elements, with context cards for partly labeled ones. A label has at most one live canvas. Opening it makes a canvas named after the label in the current project, its elements arranged into frames by concept and system; elements that become labeled later land to the right of the content (no “Newly labeled” frame yet). Deleting the label turns its live canvas into a normal canvas in the same undo step; Duplicate layout of a live canvas gives a normal canvas.
- **D-11** Adding or removing a card on a live canvas changes the label, always after confirmation. Adding a card there labels its element in the same undo step (the Entity tool without a question); removing one takes the label off the element, or off the rows and mappings that bring it there when it is a context card. Bulk adds and removing several cards are not offered. Owners, admins and modelers do this; reviewers and readers see a live canvas read-only.
- **D-20** Notes belong to the canvas and the working layer. Free notes join the frame they are dropped in; pinned notes follow their card or frame with a dashed tether. Duplicate layout copies no notes. A note joins a frame only when dropped in it (drawing, resizing or fitting a frame never moves or claims notes). A note whose card or frame leaves the canvas stays as a free note at its place, in no frame.
- **D-21** Notes are open or resolved; resolved ones can be hidden, a preference kept per person in their browser only (not on the server, not an undo step); an empty new note disappears. Every note change is one undo step; anyone who may write notes edits any note, and its panel names who wrote it.
- **D-24** Labels extend to mappings, tables and columns; the active context comes after that, as an option. Merging mappings keeps the labels of the merged ones on the mapping that stays.
- **D-25** Live canvases include tables (fully labeled ones whole, others as context) and fade unlabeled mappings. Context cards have a dashed outline, a light header and “context only”, and show their relevant rows (the Labeled filter); relevant rows are bold; mappings without the label are faded by colour, never opacity.
- **D-26** An active working context labels every model change (entities, attributes, mappings; relationships through their entities), not layout changes, in the same undo step. It stays on after reload until switched off. The context is a per-person, per-workspace preference in the browser, sent with every write; the server checks it (the label exists, the person may change labels) and records it on every change event of the group. Only owners, admins and modelers have the switch. Moving an attribute labels only the moved attribute. Deletions are not labeled; source tables, columns and concepts are not labeled by the context. A change the context would label is refused when its label is gone, and the browser switches the context off; undo and redo never label. An import labels nothing, the mappings it sends back to review included. (Extended in slice 7a.)
- **D-53** A count of notes (a card's header, a collapsed frame's block) shows the notes you would see: open ones, and resolved ones while they are shown. Notes take part in the selection: a lassoed frame stands for its free notes, Ctrl+A takes the free notes outside frames, and a pinned note moves with its card or frame, never twice.

### Projects and navigation
- **D-27** One shared model per workspace; projects organise canvases and context. Isolation between clients comes from workspaces.
- **D-28** A canvas can belong to several projects. Removing a shared canvas only takes it out of the current project; deleting a project deletes only canvases no other project uses.
- **D-29** Opening a project shows its home screen: canvas tiles with thumbnails, stats, labels (pinnable), open notes, requirements. Pinning a label to a project is saved with its change event but is not an undo step (no undo outside canvases yet). Roles without label rights see a pinned label's plain ★, not the pin control.
- **D-35** Left panel groups fold (count, sticky headers, remembered, search opens matches); “Only on this canvas”. Inspector sections fold (remembered per title); page actions always visible; lists over 20 items get a filter and “Show all”.
- **D-38** Back and forward walk one navigation history, the browser's own: the two buttons before the breadcrumbs, Alt+←/→ outside text fields, the browser's back and the mouse's back and forward buttons; the breadcrumbs stay clickable. A place is a workspace home, a project home, a canvas, or one item selected on a canvas (a card, a row, a mapping, a relationship, a frame, a note, a requirement, a label, or an entity, attribute, table or column whose card is not on the canvas); panning and zooming, the workspace home's tab, a deselection, several items, a bundle, view state and the side panels are not. A selection is a history entry of its own without an address: its place is kept for the browser tab under the entry, not in the URL, and a reload starts without a selection. Going back to a selection selects it as a click would and never opens a hidden details panel (D-54); cards, rows, frames, notes and requirement cards are centred at the same zoom when they are off screen, while mappings, relationships and labels are selected without moving the view; one on another canvas opens that canvas at its remembered view first. A field being edited is saved before Back or Forward leaves it. A place that no longer exists shows what still does, with a toast: the canvas without a selection; for a canvas no longer in its project, the project's first canvas; for a project that no longer exists, the workspace home. (Changed in slice 6b.)
- **D-39** A view-state bar at the right end of a canvas page's breadcrumbs row shows everything that hides or fades items on the canvas (Focus, layer, filters, collapsed cards, collapsed frames, hidden resolved notes, active tool), each removable with its ×, plus “Show all” from two chips the person can remove. A live canvas's “Live: {name}” chip comes first, without ×, and its Labeled filter is not counted as filtered and is kept by “Show all”. The row's height never changes: chips that do not fit fold into “+{n}”, after the breadcrumbs above the current page have shrunk; its menu lists them with their ×, while “Live: {name}” and “Show all” stay visible. Removing the filters, the collapsed cards or the collapsed frames is one undo step, and so is what “Show all” changes of them; Focus, the layer, hidden resolved notes and the tool are view state outside undo. People who cannot edit the canvas see the filter and collapsed-card chips without ×. The Changes view has a chip of its own. The Changes view and Focus exclude each other: turning one on turns the other off, and the chip says which one is on; "Show all" turns the Changes view off. (Changed in slice 6a: no lens since slice 4b-1, D-32; no role chip, the user indicator shows the role. Extended in slice 7b.)
- **D-54** Side panels: the object browser and the details panel are shown and hidden by the toolbar's buttons at its two ends, and resized by dragging their inner edge (left 200–520 px, default 264; right 280–640 px, default 340): while dragging only a guide line moves, and the panel and the canvas change size once, on release; double-click or Home resets, ←/→ change the width by 16 px, Shift by 64 px. Hidden panels and widths are remembered per person in the browser, on every page with the panels. A hidden panel stays in the page, inert. Selecting never opens a hidden details panel; actions that lead to details on purpose do (links that name what to show, menu items that edit a field in the panel, creating an element whose name is typed there, a requirement clicked in the left panel), and it stays open. Hiding, showing or resizing the left panel keeps the model where it is on the screen. A canvas's remembered view is stored with the canvas's left edge on the screen (the left panel's width; 0 when it is hidden or the window is up to 1000 px wide) and corrected by the difference when the canvas opens, in the same step that sets the view, so the model stands where it stood; views saved before slice 6a open unchanged. (Added in slice 6a.) Up to 1000 px wide both panels are hidden; a button slides one over the canvas (one at a time, not remembered), a selection does not open the details panel, and clicking an entity or a table in the left panel closes it.

### Requirements and content
- **D-30** One requirements layer with kinds: business need, data requirement, business rule, data-quality rule, non-functional. Requirements can be broken down (parent).
- **D-31** Coverage is computed from a requirement's items. A linked entity is one item, a linked attribute is one, and a linked mapping makes its attribute one. Links to tables and columns are in scope but are not items, and nothing is pulled in implicitly: an entity link does not bring its attributes. An attribute item is covered when it has an approved mapping without a type conflict. An approved mapping from a column missing from source covers no requirement (slice 7a). A retired column has left the source, so its mappings cover no requirement either (slice 7b). An entity item is covered when at least one of its attributes is covered that way. A requirement is covered when it has at least one item and all of them are covered. Coverage reads “{m} of {n} covered”, its tooltip naming the parts (“1 entity, 3 attributes”). (Changed in slice 4b-1.)
- **D-32** On the canvas: requirement markers on entity and table cards (a violet diamond and the number of requirements the card's panel lists, the list as its tooltip). Selecting a requirement colours its scope without fading anything: attribute items' rows blue when covered and orange when a gap, entity items' headers the same way, linked columns' rows and linked tables' headers blue, and linked mappings' lines drawn lit. Below 40 % a card's summary (orange when any of its items in scope is a gap) stands for its rows, as a dot at the header's right end. In a collapsed frame's block, the member's row carries the same dot. Fading the rest is always Focus (D-22), also for a requirement. (Changed in slice 4b-1.) Requirement cards (D-41) draw a dashed line with an end dot to each element their requirement is linked to on the canvas: an entity's or table's card at its header, an attribute's or column's row, a mapping's middle; into a collapsed frame, the member's row in its block. The lines show in “Everything” and are hidden in “Mappings” and “Relationships”; they dim with the other lines on a selection, never on a hover. A requirement dragged from the left panel onto a row, a card or a mapping line is linked to it at once, with Undo; onto empty canvas it places its card. Under Focus a requirement card stays lit when its requirement is the target or its scope contains the focused element itself, and a trace line when both its ends are lit. (Changed in slice 4b-2.)
- **D-33** Labels and requirements are separate; a requirement has an external reference; a label can be promoted to a requirement (later).
- **D-34** Element panels list each requirement once: direct ones first, with × to unlink, then indirect ones, without ×. An entity's come via its attributes (“via attribute”) or via mappings onto them (“via mapping”); an attribute's via mappings onto it (“via mapping”); a table's via its columns (“via column”) or via mappings that read them (“via mapping”). Mappings and columns show their own only. There is no “via entity”. Clicking a requirement opens its panel. (Changed in slice 4b-1.) Each row's “Place card” places the requirement's card next to the element (below its frame when the element is in one: a placed card never lies over a frame), or shows its card when it is on the canvas; a row dragged onto the canvas places the card where it is dropped; “Add all to this canvas” or “Add the {n} missing to this canvas” under the list. An entity or table panel whose card is not on the open canvas offers “Add to this canvas”. (Changed in slice 4b-2.)
- **D-41** Requirement card: 230 px, filled header in a reserved violet (excluded from the concept palette) with the kind's icon, the key and the kind's name; the name; a three-part coverage bar over the requirement's items (D-31): covered, mapped but not approved, not mapped (an entity item is mapped when any of its attributes has a mapping); the status badge and “{m} of {n} covered”. Below 40 % the header and a plain block. Selecting the card is selecting the requirement. A requirement has at most one card per canvas; its cards are layout, never frame members, carry no label on a live canvas and hold no pinned notes; Duplicate layout copies them. (Changed in slice 4b-2.)
- **D-42** Long texts (requirement descriptions, entity and attribute definitions, notes) use a rich-text editor dialog; panels show them formatted and shortened. Transformation rules and rule expressions stay code fields.
- **D-43** Text colour only from a named palette (warning, attention, confirmed, information).
- **D-44** New requirements get a template per kind: section headings are content, grey hints vanish when typing and are never saved, empty sections are removed on save.
- **D-45** Templates are built in now and editable per workspace later.

### Sources
- **D-55** Source import (slice 7a). A system is described once from a file in InfoMapper's source standard, a catalog query's result or DDL scripts, and re-imported the same way; the review shows everything an import would do before anything is written, and the import is one undo step. The source owns a column's name, position, type, nullable, keys and comment, and a re-import updates them; the comment is the source's and read-only (the modeller writes a note, D-57), and the review shows every overwrite. The modeller owns the classifications and the note: a re-import never changes them. The import reads every classification a file names, by code or current name; an unknown value, or a table classification named on a column, is warned about once per value and table and ignored; a new column is written with what matched; when the file differs from a column's classifications the review says so and keeps the modeller's. For new columns the file does not classify, the review suggests Business key (the only primary key column of a table with no business key, in the file or stored) and, in Data Vault 2.0 mode, Link key (a foreign key that is not a primary key); an unticked suggestion is not written, and the suggestions are part of what the review showed. A field the file leaves out is never compared or overwritten. Missing is a status, not a delete: a table, column or relationship not in the file gets “missing from source” and keeps its mappings, cards and links, and loses it when it comes back. Missing is judged only inside the scope the modeller picks: the imported schemas, or only the columns of the tables in the file; more than half of a system's columns going missing is warned about. Renames are the modeller's choice; the engine suggests one for the same position and type, and a rename keeps the column's identity. The server builds the plan again from the same text and choices and writes only when it does what the review showed; otherwise the review refreshes and says so. The working context labels nothing an import writes. A type the importer cannot read, and a table whose name differs only in case from another, are never passed over silently: the first is kept with a warning, the second left out of the import with its reason. The app has no sample files; for now one import takes up to 30,000 column rows (a limit of the local adapter, see known-limitations.md). An import raises the changes to resolve it finds and settles those the source settles (D-56); a table or column that comes back is no longer retired. (Extended in slices 7b and 7c.)
- **D-56** Changes to resolve (slice 7b). Every source change that needs a decision becomes a change to resolve, stored as it was found (kind, severity, the object, a label that survives a rename or a delete, a detail) and settled with a resolution, who and when; resolved changes stay as the system's history. The kinds: a column or a table left the source; a new column or table in a system imported before; and, for a mapping the import sent back from approved, a type conflict, a business key column that changed type or a rule rewritten for a renamed column. A business key column that changed type with no such mapping is a change of its own. A business key column is one with the Business key classification (code BK); such a change settles when its column loses the classification or the classification is removed (D-57). High when an approved mapping is affected or a business key changes type; normal otherwise. The review shows the changes and the requirements whose coverage changes before the import; afterwards they wait on the system's and table's panels, counted in the tree and the status bar. Every action shows what it does first and is one undo step: retire keeps a table or column as historical (a mapping is historical only when every input is retired; a retired input shows "until" the day it left); delete removes it from the model with its mappings, relationships and cards after showing them and the coverage change (D-47); move points the inputs that read the column at another; map to an attribute (a draft); not needed, which "Mark as needed" takes back; decide a new table column by column; approve the mapping again, only when it fits the source and following four-eyes; leave it in review; acknowledge a business key change. "Approve again all" approves the mappings one import sent back that fit and that the person may approve, and lists the others apart with the reason. A change settles itself when the source settles it (back in the source, gone before a decision) or when its object is deleted or its question answered elsewhere, each time with a resolution that says so; nothing is dropped silently. Reviewers approve again, leave in review and approve all; the other actions are for owners, admins and modelers. The changes slice 7a's imports left were derived when slice 7b was installed. The project home's model consistency and the canvas's Changes view show what source changes touch; the Changes view only fades, it changes no data or layout. (Extended in slice 7c.)
- **D-57** Classifications and notes (slice 7c). A workspace keeps one list of classifications for its source tables and columns: 19 built in from its creation (general ones and Data Vault 2.0 ones; Effective date is general; the code PII is named "Personal data") and its own. Every one can be renamed and removed and own ones added; a removed built-in one can be restored, empty, while its name is free. Built-in classifications keep a fixed code (BK, PII, LK…) that the app's rules read, so a renamed one keeps working and a removed one is absent; the rules' own texts name the role, not the classification. Names are unique per scope against every name and built-in code, compared ignoring case, spaces, `_`, `-` and `/`; a new name is also the name an import file must use. Removing one shows first where it is used and, for a built-in one a rule reads, what stops, and takes its links with it; each change is one undo step. A business key change without a mapping settles when its column loses Business key or Business key is removed. Owners, admins and modelers edit the list and set classifications from the table and column panels; reviewers, readers and an archived workspace see them read-only. With Data Vault 2.0 mode off, Data Vault classifications stay stored and are hidden in the panels and the reading; switching the mode off says how many uses stay stored. Classifications are links (AD-15, AD-18), read through one index per load; on the canvas only source rows' BK badge and the Keys filter read them (the PII badge belongs to attributes). Tables and columns have the modeller's note, plain text like a mapping's note, apart from the source's comment, which is read-only. When slice 7c was installed, the columns' business key and personal data flags became classifications and the column comments no import had written moved into the columns' notes, listed in a report. Classifications on attributes and the entity panel's Data Vault hints are not part of it.
- **D-58** Organizations and sign-in (slice 8). InfoMapper starts from nothing: on the start page a person creates an organization, with themselves as its owner and a workspace with an empty first project and canvas and the 19 built-in classifications, or signs in. Organizations are silos: nobody sees another organization's workspaces, people or names, and pages send the browser only the people of the workspace shown. Signing in asks for the e-mail address, then the organizations it has a workspace in (a list of all organizations is never shown), then the chosen organization's password; an organization's link opens its sign-in directly. A session unlocks each organization with its password once; until then its pages ask for the password and the server refuses writes into it, so adding someone to a second organization never opens the first. A signed-in person creates further organizations from the organization menu. Workspace owners and admins add people (name, e-mail, role Admin to Reader), change roles and remove people, each after a summary; only an owner gives, takes or changes the Owner role, nobody changes or removes their own row, a workspace keeps at least one owner, and an organization's owner keeps at least one workspace there. A person belongs to an organization while they have a workspace in it; removing a member or an admin from their last one ends their membership, and guests stay guests. An existing account keeps its name, shown only to an organization it is already linked to. The organization's owners and admins see its link and change its password from a People tab; people who are signed in stay signed in. People changes and password changes are change events in the workspace's log, never with the hash; like a pin (D-29) they are not undo steps. An archived workspace refuses people and password changes. Everything here runs on the local adapter in development only (AD-29, AD-31): one shared password per organization, an e-mail address reveals its organizations, a password change keeps sessions, no limit on failed sign-ins, and the development list of test users still signs in as anyone, so the silo is a rehearsal of what the database slice makes real.
- **D-59** Editing a project's name and description (slice 9). Who can edit them:
  - Owners, admins and modelers edit a project's name and description on its home, in a workspace that is not archived.
  - Reviewers and readers see them as text.

  How they save:
  - The name saves on Enter or on leaving the field; the description saves on leaving it. Both are trimmed, and the description is plain text that keeps its lines.
  - An empty name is not saved: the field goes back to the stored name and a toast says "A project needs a name.".
  - The name has no length rule, and two projects may share a name, as when a project is created.

  Undo:
  - Renaming a project is one undo step ("Rename project"), and so is changing its description ("Change project description").
  - These steps sit in the person's undo history of the workspace. It keeps the last 50 steps in the server's memory until the database slice, the same history as every other change of theirs in that workspace.
  - The home pages have no Undo or Redo. Such a step is undone and redone from any canvas of the workspace, of any project, in its turn after the steps the person made later.

  Accepted by Łukasz, 29 October 2026.
- **D-60** Typing the zoom (slice 9). The zoom value between − and + is a field. A number, with or without %, applies on Enter around the view's centre, as − and + do; a decimal, with a point or a comma, is rounded. Escape goes back. Leaving the field (a click elsewhere, Tab) applies it as Enter does. A value outside the range applies the nearest limit, 10 % or 300 %, and the field shows it; anything that is not a number goes back without a toast. The field follows every zoom except while someone types in it; keys typed in it do not reach the canvas. Typing the zoom is view state, outside undo (D-22). Accepted by Łukasz, 29 October 2026.
- **D-61** A relationship from an entity to itself (slice 10a). An entity can be related to itself (a parent and its children). Owners, admins and modelers create it with “Relate to itself” in the card header's menu, in a workspace that is not archived, and its panel opens; the relate button clicked on the same card asks for a different entity and names that item. A new one has the ends 0..1 (the parent) and 0..n (the children). It is drawn as a loop at its card's top right corner: from the right edge at the header's middle to the top edge near the corner, meeting both edges at right angles, with both ends in the chosen notation at every zoom and the label at the loop's outer point. Further loops on one card nest outside it, their ends 16 px further from the corner. The loop follows the card's moves, width and collapsing; the miniature leaves it out. Its panel names the ends “At the right end” and “At the top end”. Inside a collapsed frame it is a line inside one frame: not drawn and not counted in a bundle. Swap exchanges its two ends' cardinalities, and is refused with “Nothing to change.” when they are equal. Otherwise it behaves as any relationship: label and cardinality in its panel, delete with Undo, layers (hidden in Mappings), Focus, a history place, counted once in an entity's delete preview. Accepted by Łukasz, 29 October 2026.


### Way of working
- **D-13** UX first, architecture second. Export/import formats follow the data schema.
- **D-40** The application is built from scratch. Nothing from the old InfoMapper is migrated, neither code nor data.

## Architecture decisions (AD-xx)

### Organizations, workspaces, access
- **AD-01** A user can belong to several organizations. A workspace belongs to one organization; people from outside are invited to a workspace as guests. Note (slice 8): Until guests from other organizations are built (later, `docs/ideas.md`), a guest is a workspace member without a row in the workspace's organization; slice 8 adds no new guests, and adding a guest to another workspace of the same organization keeps them a guest (D-58).
- **AD-02** Workspaces share nothing live; patterns are copied. An organization library comes later.
- **AD-03** Model versions are named baselines (frozen snapshots) to compare with and to generate documents from. No model branches.
- **AD-04** Sign-in with e-mail and password (Supabase Auth) to start; Microsoft/Google/SSO later. Note (slice 8): Until the database slice, sign-in in development is an organization's shared password, scrypt on the server, with a signed session cookie that carries the organizations unlocked in it (D-58); Supabase Auth with a password per person replaces it.
- **AD-05** Five roles per workspace: owner, admin, modeler, reviewer (read, comment, notes, approve), reader. Reviewers write, resolve, move, pin and delete any note as modelers do; working labels are changed by owners, admins and modelers only; readers read notes and labels. Reviewers change a requirement's status only. Note (slice 8): Owners and admins add people, change roles and remove people; only an owner gives, takes or changes the Owner role, and nobody changes or removes their own row (slice 8, D-58). Note (slice 9): Owners, admins and modelers edit a project's name and description (`project.edit`), as they create projects and rename canvases; reviewers and readers do not (D-59).

### Stack and delivery
- **AD-19** One Next.js application with layers: `domain` (pure TypeScript: types, commands, validation, business logic; no framework, no database), `data` (repository interfaces + Supabase adapter), `app` (routes, screens), `ui` and `canvas` (components).

- **AD-22** GitHub, Vercel previews, two Supabase projects in the EU (dev, prod), SQL migrations in the repository.
- **AD-23** All writes go through the application server; the browser never writes to the database. Permissions, change log and validation live there.
- **AD-24** Canvas engine: **React Flow, with named workarounds** (closed 3 October 2026 after the spike and its follow-up; report in `spikes/canvas-react-flow/REPORT.md`). Rules for the canvas: (1) detail by zoom level, below 40% cards draw the header plus a plain block and lines lose their end dots; (2) no React Flow handle per row, row positions are computed from data; (3) all mapping lines in one SVG layer that reads node positions from React Flow's store; (4) the report's CSS rules: no opacity on repeated elements, no React Flow `<Background>` component (a grid is one CSS background on its own element behind the canvas pane, and must pass the pan-and-zoom regression check; slice 2a), `will-change` as in setup B; no `overflow: hidden` on repeated card elements unless the text may not fit; zoom-dependent CSS variables are set only on the elements that use them, never on the canvas root (a variable on the root restyles every card on each zoom step; found in slice 2b, S2B-14); the same holds for every element that contains the canvas, and for a class with a universal selector on the page (slice 5a, S5A-08: the side panels' widths as inherited variables on the page's grid and the prototype's `body.pdrag *` drag class cost 75–140 ms of style recalculation per switch in the dense view; the grid now gets its columns directly, and the drag has no page-wide class); a new repeated element in cards is checked with a LayerTree read against the previous slice: drawing layers must not grow with the number of cards (slice 4b-1: the requirement marker's filled pill added two compositing layers per marker by overlap). With these, all eight must-haves pass (pan and zoom 58–60 fps). Still open for slice 1, as its acceptance criteria: card resize (C-09) and hover delay (C-10), approach: switch `will-change` off during a resize drag, draw highlights in the overlay layer instead of restyling cards; row-hover dots in the overlay; clicking a card below 40% zoom. Slice 1b met C-09 and C-10 only partly. **Reference machine** (slice 2p, 8 October 2026): performance is judged on the development laptop (i7-8550U, Intel UHD 620, 1536 × 864) in the production measuring build (AD-31), A/B against the previous slice in the same sitting; there is no newer machine to measure on. **The HTML canvas line layer was tried in slice 2p and loses pan and zoom on the reference machine** (about 21–28 fps against about 55 with SVG): its script takes about 3 ms per frame, but the GPU rasterises 340 antialiased, partly dashed curves on every frame, while the SVG layer is rasterised once and moved by the compositor (tag `perf/canvas-lines-trial`, `docs/prd/slice-02p-acceptance.md`). It is no longer the planned next step; the lines stay in one SVG layer (rule 3). **Overlay and fading** (slice 4p, 15 October 2026; changed in slice 4b-1, 17 October 2026): the hover highlight, the row marks, the selection marks, the lasso and a selected requirement's coverage colours are drawn in one overlay above the cards on its own compositing layer (`will-change: transform`), so they repaint only that layer and not the canvas layer (C-10 120 → 19 ms); the hovered and lit lines are drawn there too. A mark that lies over text keeps the text at 4.5:1 (the overlay lies above it): a faint fill and a bar in the full colour where no text is. Fading (Focus) is one veil with holes at the lit cards, blocks, pinned notes and requirement cards, as one SVG path in the overlay (F4), painted into the overlay's layer with the lit lines, trace lines and rows: inside the overlay nothing composited lies below it, so it needs no layer of its own and gets none by overlap (T3, slice 4b-2: Focus adds one drawing layer instead of two). It is switched through a store outside React, never through the nodes or a canvas re-render. Slice 4b-1 replaced 4p's veil in the canvas layer with lifted cards (F1): its lift rule re-rastered the whole visible canvas layer on every switch (300–550 ms to the screen at 30 %), F4 switches in 11–88 ms. Met in slice 4b-2 (T1 in 4b-1, T3 in 4b-2, T2 dropped). Slice 4b-2's sitting (20 October 2026; three rounds; presented frames and to the screen, traced): pan and zoom with Focus on within 2.3 % of off at 30 % and 100 %, frames expanded and collapsed (S4B2-11; 4b-1's −22 to −40 % at 30 % had its pans with Focus on start from a view the pan before had zoomed in); Focus switching at most 71 ms to the screen with requirement cards lit (the lit trace lines solid: dashed, they cost about 32 ms of raster).
